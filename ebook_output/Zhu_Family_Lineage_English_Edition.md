# Hainan Wenchang Zhu Family Lineage (English Edition)

## Introduction: The Purpose of the Book
This document is the Zhu Family Lineage of Wenchang (文昌祝氏族谱). It was painstakingly compiled between 2004 and 2007 by a 16th-generation elder named Zhu Zhaoke, after he retired. Because many older records were destroyed or lost over time, he and a few relatives traveled from village to village in Hainan, visiting ancestral tombs and interviewing elders to piece the fragmented family tree back together.
The book serves three purposes:
To solve the mystery of exactly where the Wenchang branch of the Zhu family came from.
To honor ancestors and revolutionary martyrs who died in the 20th-century wars.
To provide future generations—especially those who may no longer read Chinese or live in the ancestral villages—with a clear record of who they are and where they came from.

## Part 1: Deep Ancestry and Origins (The Roots)
The document traces the Zhu (祝) surname back to the mythological origins of Chinese civilization.
The Ancient Era: The surname is connected to the Yellow Emperor and his descendant Zhurong, who served as the official of fire in ancient times. When King Wu established the Zhou Dynasty (around 1046 BC), he granted land in what is now Shandong province to establish the "State of Zhu." The descendants took the state's name as their surname.
The Golden Age: Over centuries, the most prominent branch of the family settled in Taiyuan (Shanxi province). The book proudly notes that the Zhu family produced many famous historical figures, including scholars, calligraphers (like the famous Ming dynasty artist Zhu Yunming), and virtuous officials.

## Part 2: Migration to Hainan (The Journey South)
Like many families in Southeast Asia, your ancestors were part of a massive southward migration.
Entering Hainan: In the early Ming Dynasty (around the 1300s or 1400s), an ancestor named Dehui traveled from Fujian province to serve as a government official in Wanning, Hainan Island.
The Wenchang Branch: Several generations later, the family split into different branches. The founding ancestor of your specific branch, Zhu Gao (祝高), moved north from Wanning and settled in Gaolong Village, Wenchang. Zhu Gao is recognized as the "1st Generation" (一世) of the Wenchang Zhu family.

## Part 3: Your Place in the Lineage (The "Ming" Generation)
Chinese families use a "Generation Poem" (字辈, Zibei) to name their children. This is a pre-written poem where each character represents a successive generation. When you see your name in this book, the first character of your given name dictates exactly which generation you belong to.

The book records your name, Mingcan (明灿 / 铭灿), alongside your generational brothers and cousins (such as Mingwei, Mingcheng, Minghui, and Mingjie). In Chinese, the character Ming (明/铭) generally means bright, clear, or engraved in memory, while Can (灿) translates to glorious or radiant.
Because the generation markers are strictly followed, any other Zhu family member you meet around the world with the "Ming" character in their name is your generational peer, regardless of your actual ages. The men with "Sheng" (圣) are your father's generation, "Jia" (家) your grandfather's, and "Chao" (朝) your great-grandfather's.

To fully appreciate the weight of this book and your place within it, there are a few more profound cultural and historical details hidden in the text that you should know. This book is not just a record of the past; it contains a century-old promise, tracks a massive historical migration, and even leaves a direct set of instructions for you and your generation.
Here is what else you need to know about your family lineage and this document:
1. You Are Part of the "Xia Nanyang" (下南洋) Diaspora
Your lineage book physically documents the famous Hainanese diaspora. In the 19th and 20th centuries, millions of people from southern China migrated to Southeast Asia to escape poverty and war—a historical event known as Xia Nanyang (Journey to the Southern Ocean).
The author made a concerted effort to track down family members who left Hainan. Next to certain names in the visual diagrams, there are specific geographic tags. For example:
Malaysia (马来西亚): Relatives like Shengli (圣利) and Jiahua (家华) are recorded as having moved to Malaysia.
Singapore (新加坡): Relatives like Chaoxue (朝学) and the "Jianhua Shop" (建华店) are listed in Singapore.
Vietnam (越南): A relative named Jia'an (家安) is noted as residing in Vietnam.
Even if family members left the ancestral village of Gaolong (高隆村) generations ago, the book ensures they—and their descendants—are never written out of the family history.
2. Your Immediate Family Cluster in the Book
Because the plain text stripped away the visual lines, it can be hard to see exactly who is standing next to you in the tree. In the original PDF (on pages 44/45), your name, Mingcan (明灿), is clustered directly alongside your brothers and close cousins.
The book lists you in this exact grouping:
The "Ming" Brothers/Cousins: Mingwei (明伟), Mingxun (明训), Mingcheng (明诚), Mingjian (明坚), Mingcan (明灿), and Mingtai (明泰).
Female Relatives: Listed right beside you are Xiaoyan (小燕), Xiaoling (晓玲), and Xiaoyun (晓云).
The Previous Generation: You are listed immediately under the 18th-generation elders, specifically near Shengjin (圣进), who is likely your father or a close uncle in your direct paternal line.
3. The Book Fulfills a 100-Year-Old Promise
One of the most touching parts of this document is the preface from 1895 (the 21st year of the Guangxu reign). At that time, an ancestor named Zhu Xinchuan realized that the Wenchang branch of the family was losing touch with the original Wanzhou (Wanning) branch.
He wrote a pact stating that whenever either side updated their genealogy, they must gift a copy to the other so that "generations of descendants reading this genealogy will know they share the exact same origin." However, wars, revolutions, and time disrupted this. It wasn't until 2007—over 110 years later—that the author of this current book (Zhu Zhaoke) traveled to Wanning, found the ancestral hall, reconnected with the Wanzhou relatives, and finally fulfilled that 1895 promise by completing this unified lineage.

## 4. A Direct Command to Your Generation
The book ends its introduction with a strict mandate for the future. The author, having spent his retirement years traveling from village to village to save this history from being lost, left a specific instruction for the descendants:
"世世代代，继续努力，隔二、三十年，续修一次。告慰先祖，启蒙后代。"
(Generation after generation, continue the effort; update it every 20 or 30 years. To comfort the ancestors, and enlighten the descendants.)
Because this book was finalized and printed in 2007, the 20-year mark is approaching right now. The elders who compiled this document have passed the torch. It is now the responsibility of the 19th and 20th generations—you and your "Ming" and "Yun" generation peers—to ensure that marriages, new births, and the continued journeys of the Zhu family are recorded for the next update.

To commemorate their former state and Zhurong, the original nobles of the State of Zhu took the state's name as their surname, thus giving rise to the Zhu surname.
